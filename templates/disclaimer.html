Before you use this

DiresQ was built in three days by two students at Katy Youth Hacks 2026. It has never been used in a real disaster.

It does not call for help

DiresQ does not contact 911, emergency services, or any agency. Filing a report puts a card on a screen that other volunteers may or may not be looking at.

If someone is in danger, call 911 first.

The triage helper is not medical advice

It runs START, a real protocol used at real mass-casualty scenes, and it tells you who to reach first. It does not tell you what to do when you get there, it does not diagnose anyone, and it does not replace a clinician.

It can return Deceased. Software written by teenagers over a weekend is not a competent judge of whether someone has died. Treat that as a reason to get a qualified person there, never as a conclusion.

Nothing you type into the triage helper is stored. The answers produce a category and are discarded — never written to the database, never attached to anyone. They concern a person in no position to consent to being recorded.

Nobody is verified

Anyone can sign up and mark themselves a responder. No ID checks, no affiliation, no vetting. A name on the board means somebody typed that name — not that they are trained, insured, equipped, or who they say they are.

Locations are self-reported

Coordinates come from the browser or a tap on a map. Someone can sit at home and claim to be on scene. We flag inconsistency — being over 500 m away when you say you have arrived — but a determined person can still lie. Do not treat a pin as proof anybody is anywhere.

A red row means silence, not danger

The overdue timer says nobody has heard from that person recently. That is all it says. Someone can check in while trapped, and someone can go quiet because their phone died. It tells you when to start asking.
