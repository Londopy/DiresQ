# AI usage disclosure — draft for the authors to correct

This is a statement about *your* process, so the final wording has to be yours.
What follows is a draft that is accurate as far as I can tell from inside the
work. **Read it critically — if any clause is not true, change it.** A
disclosure that overstates your involvement is worse than no disclosure, and a
disclosure that understates it is the thing it exists to prevent.

---

## Draft statement

> **Use of generative AI.** The prose of this paper was drafted with substantial
> assistance from Claude (Anthropic). The authors set the research question,
> selected the venue and the sources, directed the structure and argument of
> every section, and made all design decisions described in §3. The model
> retrieved and read sources, drafted section text from them, and identified
> several errors in the authors' own claims — including an overstatement about
> the escalation mechanism in §3 and an incorrect count in the abstract. All
> source material cited was obtained and read during this process; quotations
> were extracted from the source texts rather than reproduced from memory. The
> authors have reviewed and verified the content and take full responsibility
> for it.

## Clauses to check before you sign your name to this

**"the authors… selected the sources"** — partly true. You named the initial
four-paper list. Smith et al. (2018), the FEMA doctrine, and the Fritz &
Mathewson original were found during the work, not chosen by you in advance.
Consider: *"selected or approved the sources."*

**"All source material cited was obtained and read during this process"** —
**currently false.** Liu (2014), Auferbauer & Tellioğlu (2017) and Kankanamge et
al. (2019) are cited from their abstracts. Either read them, or change this
clause to something like: *"Three sources are cited only for positions stated in
their abstracts; this is marked in the text."* Do not leave the sentence as it
stands.

**"The authors have reviewed and verified the content"** — true only once you
have actually read the paper end to end, and once the second author has too. It
is a claim with a date on it. Do not write it before it is true.

**"identified several errors in the authors' own claims"** — true, and worth
keeping. It is more informative than a generic acknowledgement and it makes the
statement read as a description rather than a formality.

## On where this goes

Venues differ. JOSS requires a disclosure naming tools, scope of assistance, and
confirmation of human review, and treats non-disclosure as an ethics breach.
ISCRAM and SocArXiv have no fixed requirement at time of writing. **Include it
anyway.** The cost is three sentences; the cost of omitting it and being asked
later is the paper.

Placement: an "Acknowledgements" or "Statement on AI use" section immediately
before the references is conventional and adequate.

---

## A note you did not ask for

You will be asked, at some point, whether you wrote this. The honest answer is
that you did the work — the system, the reasoning, the judgement about what was
true — and that the sentences were drafted with a tool, under your direction,
and checked by you. That is a defensible position and a common one. It stops
being defensible the moment the checking has not happened.
